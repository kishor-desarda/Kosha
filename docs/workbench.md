# The workbench

Ways to get around Kosha quickly (specification 9.1). Everything here is personal: it changes how Kosha looks and
what it lists for you, never a part, file or BOM.

## Ctrl+K: go anywhere

Press **Ctrl+K** (⌘K on a Mac), or **Go to** at the top of the screen. Type a part number, a file name, a change
number or a few words; choose with ↑ ↓ and press Enter. Without typing you see:

- **This page:** *Pin this page* and *Save this view*;
- **Favourites**, **Saved views**, **Pinned** pages and the pages you opened **Recently**;
- every **Screen** you may open.

## Favourites

The star next to the title of a file, part, BOM, change or project adds it to your favourites. Favourites show their
current name and state, so a renamed part shows its new name. A record you may no longer see, or one that was
deleted, drops out of the list (and comes back if it is restored).

## Saved views

*Save this view* keeps the screen you are on with its filters (for example the Explorer filtered to released files)
under a name such as *Released pump drawings*. Saving under the same name replaces the view. Delete a view from
Ctrl+K with its bin button.

## Pinned pages

*Pin this page* gives the page a tab in the strip at the bottom of the screen, for the few pages you keep going back
to during the day (up to 8). The cross on a tab unpins it.

## Undo

Deleting files, removing a favourite, unpinning a page and deleting a view show a message with **Undo** for ten
seconds. Deleted files also wait in the recycle bin, where they can be put back later.

## Look

Account menu › Theme also sets the **accent colour** (blue, teal, green, purple or orange; high contrast keeps its own)
and the **text size** (smaller, normal, larger, largest). Every accent meets WCAG AA in the light and the dark theme.
Lifecycle states carry a shape as well as a colour (a pencil for in work, a tick for released, a cross for obsolete).

## Where it is kept

Favourites, views, accent, text size and pins are saved with your account and follow you to other computers.
Recent pages stay in the browser. A personal-data export lists them; erasing a person removes them.
